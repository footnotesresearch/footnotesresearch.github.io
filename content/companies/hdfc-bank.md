HDFC Bank is India's largest private-sector bank. It takes deposits from about 100 million customers through roughly 9,700 branches and lends to households, small businesses and large companies. At March 2026 it had ₹31.05 lakh crore of deposits and ₹29.37 lakh crore of loans. Retail lending makes up a little over half of the book, and home loans are the largest part of that at ₹8.89 lakh crore.

Most of its income is NII (net interest income): the gap between what it earns on loans and investments and what it pays on deposits and borrowings. The rest comes from fees, mainly cards, payments, transaction banking and selling insurance and investment products, plus treasury gains. It also owns stakes in the group's insurance, asset management, broking and consumer-finance companies. HDB Financial Services, the consumer-finance arm, listed in July 2025. The bank's ADRs (American depositary receipts) trade on the New York Stock Exchange.

The biggest change in its recent history was the merger with its parent, the mortgage lender HDFC Ltd, effective 1 July 2023. HDFC Ltd brought a large home-loan book that it had funded mostly with bonds and other borrowings, not with current and savings accounts. The merged bank therefore started with more loans than deposits and a heavy load of borrowings. Since then it has been raising deposits faster than it lends and repaying that inherited debt.

FY26 also brought a governance event: the part-time chairman resigned in March 2026, citing concerns about practices at the bank. An external legal review followed, and a new chairman had been appointed by July 2026. Our takeaways set out what the filings and calls say about each of these threads.
