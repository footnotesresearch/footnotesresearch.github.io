## What we checked

We read HDFC Bank's integrated annual reports for FY24, FY25 and FY26 and four management call transcripts filed with the exchanges:

- an unscheduled investor call on 19 March 2026, the day after the chairman resigned;
- the Q3 FY26 earnings call (17 January 2026);
- the Q4 FY26 earnings call (18 April 2026);
- the Q1 FY27 earnings call (18 July 2026).

We took every figure from the standalone financial statements, the Directors' Report or the notes, and recorded the page for each. All derived figures were computed from those inputs and are in the accompanying data file:

- LDR (loan-to-deposit ratio);
- borrowings as a share of total liabilities;
- the run-off of debt inherited from HDFC Ltd against its stated maturity schedule;
- growth rates and price changes.

We then compared what management said on the calls with what the annual reports later showed.

All figures are standalone. ₹1 crore is ₹10 million; ₹1 lakh crore is ₹1 trillion.

### Interpretation (labelled as such)

These are our readings of the figures above, not statements made in the filings.

- **Funding trend.** The funding numbers point the way management said they would. LDR and borrowings have come down every year since the merger, and HDFC Ltd's legacy debt has run off faster than its stated schedule. The CASA ratio and NIM have not recovered alongside them, so the cost of replacing borrowings with deposits has so far shown up as time-deposit growth.
- **Matching gains and provisions.** In two of the three post-merger years, a large disposal gain arrived in the same year as a floating provision of similar size. Floating provisions are created at the board's discretion, within a board-approved limit. Read together, these pairings smooth reported profit growth. The filings do not say whether the two were linked.

## What the filings leave open

- **The legal review.** The bank has published only a one-sentence summary of the external law firms' findings. The filings do not say whether the full report, or a fuller summary, will be released, or which "happenings and practices" were examined.
- **The audit-trail gap.** The filings do not explain why database-level logging on the core banking system was enabled only on 4 March 2026. They also do not say what controls covered direct database changes before that date, or whether any review of that period was done.
- **The DFSA notice.** The decision notice says what the DIFC branch may no longer do, but the filings do not give the DFSA's reasons or any financial exposure linked to the complaints.
- **The FY27 LDR target.** Management has not said whether the 85% to 90% range given in January 2026 still stands, after describing the ratio in April as no longer a binding constraint.
- **Why legacy debt fell faster than scheduled.** The filings do not say whether HDFC Ltd's borrowings were prepaid, refinanced into other borrowings, or simply matured on a different profile from the one disclosed.
- **The floating provisions.** The filings do not explain how the size of the FY24 and FY26 floating provisions was set, or whether either was linked to that year's disposal gain.
- **Leadership succession.** On the March call an analyst noted that the CEO's term was due in about seven months. In July 2026 the bank said the reappointment was "work in progress" with the board and its nomination committee. The documents we reviewed do not record an outcome.
- **Expected credit loss accounting.** The bank says its provisions look adequate for the switch to ECL (expected credit loss) accounting from 1 April 2027 and expects "nothing material". The filings do not quantify the impact.
